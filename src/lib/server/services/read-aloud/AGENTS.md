# Read Aloud (server)

Parent rules: `src/lib/server/services/AGENTS.md`. Read aloud turns a finished reply into speech through the speech service in `deploy/speech-service/`, whose README is the contract. The browser builds the speech script; this folder checks it, opens the session, and serves the service's audio and timeline back. Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-16-read-aloud-design.md` (§6).

## Rules

- **The address** comes only from `getReadAloudUrl()` in `config-store.ts` (`TTS_ENABLED` and `TTS_URL`; empty means off). `speech-client.ts` takes it as a parameter and reads no configuration. Nothing a model writes becomes an address (ADR-0068). Held by `config-store.test.ts`.
- **This server is the service's only reader.** A session has one reader of each stream for its whole life, and `Range` changes nothing, so `readings.ts` opens `audio` and `events` once each, as soon as the session exists, and serves every browser request from an in-memory replay. Never forward a browser's `Range` upstream, and never reopen a stream that ended: a second GET is 409. Held by `readings.test.ts` ("a second reader of the audio hears it from the first byte", "does not close for a reader that left early").
- **No timeout on the two streams.** Both stay silent while playback is paused, and undici gives up on a body silent for 300 s, so the streams are read with `node:http`. The short calls (create, position, delete) use `fetch` with a time limit.
- **One reading per person.** `openReading` closes the person's current reading first (`event: replaced` to its events readers) and runs two quick presses one after the other. A press takes a place in line (`takeOpenTicket`) the moment its request arrives, before the body is read, and `openReading` refuses one whose place is older than one that has opened (`ReadingSuperseded`, the route's 409 `superseded`), because two quick presses finish their checks in any order. A session id is only its owner's. Held by `readings.test.ts` ("opening a second reading for the same person…", "two people read at once", "serves two quick presses…", "keeps the later press when the earlier one finishes opening last") and the route test "takes its place in line before it reads or checks anything".
- **The replay.** Audio from the first byte, then live, up to `AUDIO_REPLAY_MAX_BYTES` (8 MB, about 8.7 minutes): past it `attachAudio` answers null (the route's 409 `already_read`) and readers already attached keep their place. The timeline is kept whole. A heartbeat comment every 15 s goes to waiting events readers and never into the replay. A reader detaches in its stream's `cancel()`; never rely on `request.signal`. Held by `readings.test.ts` ("stops replaying after its cap", "keeps the heartbeat out of the replay…").
- **The browser's events stream outlives the service's** (decision 8, early review R7). The service's `done` means the voice has finished making sentences, not that anyone has finished listening, and a reading another tab or device starts meanwhile must reach this one as `event: replaced`. So a clean end of the service's timeline (`done`, or its own `error` event) is recorded (`timelineEnded`) and never ends the browser's stream: it stays open with its heartbeat until the reading closes, and then ends (after `replaced`, when a press replaced it). A timeline the service cut still fails its readers. Held by `readings.test.ts` ("tells the events reader of a reading whose synthesis has finished that it was replaced", "keeps sending the heartbeat after done…").
- **The idle end.** A reading with no reader of either stream for 30 s (`READING_IDLE_MS`, counted from its creation too) closes: an events reader counts, since the browser keeps its stream open while it plays what it has. It also closes when the audio and the timeline have ended, one reader was given all the audio, an events reader has come at least once and no reader of either stream is left (the browser closes its events stream when the reading is over, so that is the drain, and there is nothing to DELETE; it asks for the events a moment after the audio, and on a busy machine the audio can be read to its end first, so a drain waits for the events reader to have come, and a client that never opens it is left to the idle end); a reader that left early (Safari's probe) is not that. Held by `readings.test.ts` ("ends a reading 30 s after…", "counts the idle time from creation…", "closes once the audio has ended…", "keeps a drained reading while its events reader is attached…", "does not end a reading as idle while its events reader is attached", "does not close a drained reading before its events reader has come").
- **Closing** aborts both upstream fetches, sends `DELETE`, ends every reader and forgets the reading. Never `DELETE` because the service said `done`: it means synthesis finished, not playback.
- **Containment.** A session request is read aloud only when every written word occurs in the reply's text, in order, and its tokens are the shared dictionary's reading of it (`containment.ts`, with `src/lib/shared/read-aloud/spoken-forms.ts`). List numbers are not searched. Held by `containment.test.ts`, and for the route by `src/routes/api/read-aloud/sessions/sessions.test.ts`.
- **A Document** (the request names `document: { artifactId, version }` and the panel's chat instead of a message). It is read only for a person the panel itself would show it to, and its words are checked against that version's own stored text: both through `getDocumentVersionBody`, the artifacts facade's scoped read of one version by its number, so another person's Document, an incognito chat's from outside it, a version it does not have and any kind but a Document answer the same 404 `not_found`, and words the version does not hold answer 422 `not_in_document` with a log line of counts only. Held by `src/routes/api/read-aloud/sessions/sessions.document.test.ts`, which runs the real service on an in-memory database.
- **A standard report** (an Atlas report; the request names `report: { fileId }`, spec §4.13). It is read only through the preview's own read of that chat file (`readStandardReportHtml` in `generated-file-serving.ts`: `resolveGeneratedFileServing` in preview mode, so the same person, file and chat as the panel's preview, and only a file served under the `standard-report` profile, the one whose preview runs scripts; it reads the report as written, without the bridge the preview adds); anything else answers 404 `not_found`. Its words are checked against what its markup shows (`reportKey`: comments, scripts, styles, templates and drawings out, every tag with its attributes out, entities decoded; the renderer escapes every `<` of its text), and words it does not show answer 422 `not_in_report` with a log line of counts only. The browser reads the report from its own inert copy, never from the frame (`src/lib/client/read-aloud/AGENTS.md`). Held by `containment.test.ts` ("the containment check for a report"), `generated-file-serving.test.ts` ("readStandardReportHtml") and `src/routes/api/read-aloud/sessions/sessions.report.test.ts`.
- **Privacy.** No audio and no reply text is written to disk or to a log. A log line carries a session id, counts, byte counts, a status or a field path. Held by `readings.test.ts` and the route test "422 not_in_reply, and the log line carries no reply text".
- **Voices.** The built-in voices are configuration in code (`voices.ts`). A personal voice is a built-in voice plus five knob values and a name, and it is its maker's only: every query in `personal-voices.ts` carries the signed-in person's id (`readAloudVoices.userId` or `users.id`), and no function takes another person as its subject. The choice is resolved per language at each reading (`resolveReadingVoices`): the person's choice when it is a built-in voice of that language or their own voice of it, else the language's default, so another person's id, a deleted id and a voice whose base is gone all read as the default. A deleted chosen voice gives the choice to its base, in the same transaction. Knob values are stored as slider values and made into Zonos controls only by `knobsToControls` (`$lib/shared/read-aloud/voice-knobs`, through `resolvedVoice`), so a range that is measured again reaches saved voices too. Held by `personal-voices.test.ts` ("never lists, changes, deletes or reads another person's voice", "deleting the chosen voice gives the choice back to its base", "a choice is of its own language", "a voice whose base is gone…") and `voices.test.ts`; through the routes, by `src/routes/api/read-aloud/voices/voices.test.ts` ("answers 404 for another person's voice and 400 for choosing it"). The routes (`voices`, `voices/{id}`, `preferences`) are adapters: the bodies are `voice-requests.ts`'s zod schemas (knobs are exactly the five keys, each a finite number inside its slider), and a service answer of null is 404 `not_found`, `UnknownVoice` 400 `unknown_voice`, `TooManyVoices` 409 `too_many`.
- **Preview** (the editor's and Settings' ▶, `preview.ts`, the route `POST /api/read-aloud/preview`). One preview per person: a newer one aborts the older one's calls upstream and the older one answers 409 `superseded` (`renderPreview` keeps the person's preview in the making; a preview of one person never touches another's). One speech-service call per sentence (`previewSpeech`, all at once), joined with a 0.45 s gap (the service's `SPEECH_GAP_SECONDS`, kept equal by hand) into one MP3 whose sentences' times go in `X-Read-Aloud-Sentences` (`mp3-join.ts` reads and joins frames; it refuses any clip that is not MPEG-1 Layer III, 44.1 kHz, mono). Nothing is cached, and nothing is logged but the kind of failure, a status and a field: never a word of the sample or of the person's own words. The address comes only from `getReadAloudUrl()`. Held by `preview.test.ts` ("a later preview of the same person supersedes the earlier one", "two people's previews do not touch each other"), `mp3-join.test.ts` and the route test `src/routes/api/read-aloud/preview/preview.test.ts` ("503 voice_unavailable when the service fails, and the log line carries no text").

## The fake (`testing/fake-speech-service.ts`)

A real loopback HTTP server that follows the README: one reader of each stream (409 for a second, 404 for an unknown session), `Range` ignored, sentences made only while `i <= position + 2`, times on the stream's clock with the 0.025 s encoder delay, 422 with a field path and never a value, 413 over 4 MiB, an ended session kept for a late events reader. Options: `secondsPerChar`, `firstAudioDelayMs`, `refuseSessions`, `failAtSentence`, `cutAtSentence`, `errorDelayMs` (wait before an error or a cut, so the sentences before it have started playing: a real failure comes after audio began, and the browser needs about 3 s of a stream with no length before it plays), `wordTimes`, `failPreview` (`"unavailable"` answers a preview 503, `"failed"` 502, at once) and `previewDelayMs` (a preview is answered after this wait; a caller that goes away meanwhile is listed in `abortedPreviews`, and every accepted preview body in `previews`); `POST /v1/preview` follows the README (the body is checked like the service checks it, and the answer is one whole MP3 with a length: a 10-byte ID3v2 tag and silent frames as long as the text); `setOptions` replaces them as a whole. It imports Node built-ins and relative files only, so Playwright specs may import it. Where the README and Plan 2's wording differ, the README wins. `scripts/run-fake-speech-service.ts` runs it on a port by hand.
